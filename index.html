About

I'm a Mechanical Engineering graduate student at the University of Toledo (M.S., expected December 2026), with a background that spans experimental fluid mechanics, thermal systems, CAD, and hands-on fabrication.

My current research investigates the aerodynamic drag of particles in supersonic flow, a problem that sits at the intersection of high-speed gas dynamics and multiphase flow. I designed and built the supersonic wind tunnel facility this work runs on, and developed its experimental techniques comprising of Schlieren imaging for flow visualization and PIV/PTV for velocity field measurement, from optical layout through calibration and validation. I also developed the facility's LabVIEW data acquisition and control system, integrating pressure and temperature instrumentation, automated valve sequencing, safety interlocks, and real-time monitoring, and I process the resulting datasets through Python and MATLAB analysis pipelines.

Before Toledo, I earned my B.E. in Mechanical Engineering from Kathmandu University (GPA 3.5/4.0), where I worked across a range of hands-on projects, from fabricating a pipe-cleaning crawler robot to modeling a hydrogen fuel cell powertrain retrofit that led to a peer-reviewed publication in IOP Conference Series (2023).

One of the formative experiences of my undergraduate years was leading Team Junkiri in the Shell Eco-Marathon Asia, an international competition where student teams design and build ultra-efficient vehicles. I led the SolidWorks chassis and surface modeling, coordinated fabrication across a multi-person team, and managed the logistics of competing in Indonesia. On the powertrain side, we retrofitted the vehicle's lawnmower engine with an electronic fuel injection (EFI) system in adherence to competition guidelines and manually tuned it until the engine ran at its most efficient state. More than the result, it was an education in what it takes to take a design from a screen to a physical object under real constraints.

I'm based in Toledo, OH and graduating in December 2026, open to relocation for the right opportunity in mechanical design, thermal and fluid systems, R&D, or EV/clean energy engineering.

Research

MS thesis · Fluids and Imaging Innovation Lab · University of Toledo · 2025 to 2026

Coefficient of drag in particle-laden supersonic flow

Advisor: Dr. Omid Amili · Fluids and Imaging Innovation Lab

My thesis investigates how particles behave aerodynamically in supersonic gas flows, a problem with direct relevance to solid rocket propulsion, powder deposition systems, and high-speed particulate transport. Working under Dr. Omid Amili, I designed and built the supersonic wind tunnel facility this research runs on, and developed its experimental techniques, Schlieren imaging to visualize shock structures and PIV/PTV to map velocity fields around particles, from optical layout through calibration and validation. The raw multi-modal datasets are processed through Python and MATLAB pipelines from acquisition through to drag coefficient extraction.

I also developed the facility's data acquisition and control system in LabVIEW, integrating pressure and temperature instrumentation, automated valve sequencing, safety interlocks, and real-time monitoring, all documented in full specifications and test reports, which gave me direct experience with the complete design-to-hardware cycle in a research environment.

Note: the Schlieren images shown here are preliminary. The imaging system is still being fine-tuned.
