How hard does a supersonic jet push on a solid particle? Measuring that drag inside the shock cells of an underexpanded jet, on a facility and imaging system I built.

The problem
  • Drag correlations for solid particles in supersonic flow disagree by a factor of two at the conditions that matter for solid rocket propulsion, cold-spray deposition, and high-speed particulate transport, and nearly all the data behind them come from shock tubes and ballistic ranges. The thesis measures drag in the complementary configuration, the quasi-steady shock-cell field of an underexpanded supersonic jet.
Facility & imaging
  • Designed and built the facility: a 5.08 mm converging nozzle fed through a conditioned settling chamber, with a LabVIEW data acquisition and control system for pressure and temperature instrumentation, automated valve sequencing, safety interlocks, and real-time monitoring.
  • Developed the facility's experimental techniques, Schlieren imaging for flow visualization and PIV/PTV for velocity measurement, from optical layout through calibration and validation. A Z-type schlieren system images the jet at 34,035 frames per second, resolving the shock train and individual bead silhouettes at once.
  • Inferred the gas state from the shock-cell spacing measured by autocorrelation (fully expanded Mach 1.60, 447 m/s), and tracked beads of 342–675 μm into nozzle-referenced velocity and acceleration histories with a Python/MATLAB pipeline.
Analysis ongoing
  • Separating the non-drag forces experimentally by regressing acceleration on 1/dp across particle sizes, which splits it into a drag group and a size-independent pressure-gradient group; drag-coefficient analysis at Rep ≈ 20,000–40,000 is ongoing, for comparison against the Henderson, Loth et al., and Clift–Gauvin correlations.
